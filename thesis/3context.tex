\section{Motivation}

An agent acting in its environment receives observational input, and responds to it with a commanded control input. 
Differently said, the agent is a function in relation to its environment, and to find an optimal behavior, we need
to approximate this optimal function as closely as possible. While Neural Networks are known to be universal approximators
\needcite{#4 summary, #5 theorem 2.2}, and hence able to approximate any function, in the case of embodied agent, different
constraints need to be taken into account. \needcite{#1, around formula 3} names additionally to external embodiment constraints
possible intrinsic constraints, and argues that such can even be "beneficial" for the control structure. Additionally, less parameters
for deployment are helpful due to energy usage and storage capacity, the core of the notion of cheap design\needcite{TODO}.
So, for such embodied agents, the goal is to find an "embodied universal approximator" \needcite{#2, page 3}, which restricts the neural network
architecture to an intrinsic, unknown dimension, encoding physical properties belonging to the agent and the environment, while disallowing impossible
movements \needcite{#2, chap 3}.

Biology motivates this approach even further: Here it is clear to see, that by commanding a body, some features of the control architecture can be
simplified and delegated to the embodiment\needcite{TODO}. For example, \needcite{#9}'s passive walker clearly shows, that some movements can be
produced by the body without any control input, just by choosing the right embodiment. While obviously here being simplified to not include any
control at all, it can quite simply be extended with such \needcite{#9, reference to his previous paper}. \needcite{#10, p.4} mentions such
combination, as a robot climbing up walls, whose design was inspired by geckos, indeed using energy for control, however very little compared to
an approach not exploiting these specific embodiment design. Further, it can be seen that the complex behaviors are not only less energy intensive
to produce, but also utilize muscle synergies \needcite{#12} and thus fewer control parameters. And while it is difficult to clearly attribute the effect of the embodiment \needcite{#11},
for us, this gives us the reason to believe that the simplicity, and thus efficiency, comes from a low-dimensionality of the controller \needcite{#2}.
