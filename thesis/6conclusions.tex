\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In this thesis, we have shown that simply enforcing low-dimensional structures or latent layers does not
improve the performance of controllers for overparameterized systems; instead, it might even hurt performance. However,
when combining sufficiently pretrained latent projectors with the right controller architecture, they can
achieve similar performances to fully connected networks, while being more efficient in their parameter count
and training time. While this could be clearly shown for LQR, under RL only tendencies were observed, requiring
further investigation.

We further found conflicting results on whether low-dimensional latent states
can improve robustness to measurement noise. While LQR solvers and model-informed
\emph{StE} present a tendency here, it is insignificant compared to the spread of the seeds.
For the camera environment, we could not observe any of that. This, however, might
be related to the first point, where our RL approaches already showed bad performance
under the clean loop.

Regarding the latent state, it is also important to ensure that not only control information is carried
here, but also information about the observed state. Otherwise, robustness to control noise might worsen.

In relation to that, we also note that a low degradation under control noise can also hint at a barely acting
controller, and is not always a sign of robustness. Still, in our experiments, low-dimensional controllers
were affected slightly more by it than fully parameterized ones, which we observed across all experiments.

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

We have presented that combining pretrained encoders with a trainable network
can reduce the parameter count effectively on a toy example, while noting common problems in relation to
architecture design. Failing to translate its success to a vision-based problem brought
additional pitfalls to light. Further, we presented that fully parameterized controllers offer, if only slightly, more
robustness to control noise, while for the other noise types we could only observe tendencies,
which open possibilities for further research here.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Throughout our experiments, we noticed multiple times how variable metrics across seeds are. While we intended to mitigate
this by using multiple seeds, in some cases relying only on the median might not be as expressive as it should be. Additionally,
by excluding diverging seeds, we might have unintentionally improved the values for these controllers.

What is also important to note is the setup for \cref{chap:perception}. Here, due to the simplicity of the camera setup, as well as
the extrapolation of learning rates instead of calibration, the quality of the results has suffered. Given the high percentage of
exits during episodes here, as well as non-functional controllers using pretrained encoders, it is difficult to draw much from these
experiments. While these decisions were mostly caused by time constraints of this thesis, it would be interesting to repeat these experiments
at a more appropriate complexity in the future.

\section{Future Research}
\label{sec:future-research}

From where this thesis ends, there are different directions, which we have left for future research
and discussion. These include, but are not limited to, whether:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item and how the number of parameters can be distributed more effectively between
	the extractor and the actor, to achieve performances comparable to a fully connected network;
	\item improving the imaging setup leads to better position and velocity encodings for fully trained networks;
	\item our results hold for more complex systems, e.g., a walking humanoid robot;
	\item these findings are transferable to real-world applications, i.e., if this setup
	were built and tested.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, we also want to mention that, while these are interesting directions, the data collected
in our experiments permits additional analysis. For example,
while we captured the compute time and environment steps needed, a different angle could include
this in the evaluation of the approaches. Taken together with the limitations mentioned, we believe
that while we found low-dimensional controllers to perform worse,
there is more research to be done on this topic.
