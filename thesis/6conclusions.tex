\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

In this thesis we have shown that simply enforcing low-dimensional structures, or latent layers does not
improve the performance of controllers for overparameterized systems, instead it hurts performance. However,
when combining sufficiently pretrained latent projectors with the right controller architecture, they can
achieve similar performances to fully-connected networks, while being more efficient in their parameter count
and training time. While this could be clearly shown for LQR, under RL only tendencies were observed, requiring
further investigation.

We further found low-dimensional latent states to improve robustness to measurement noise, across all of our
experiment implementations. Additionally, a lower count in parameters can improve this robustness even more.

Regarding the latent state, it is also important to ensure that not only control information is carried
here, but also information about the observed state. Otherwise, robustness to control noise is compromised.
In relation to that, we also note that a low degradation in control noise can also hint at a barely acting
controller, and is not always a sign of robustness.

Finally, we note that there might also be a slight robustness improvement in relation to parametric uncertainty
for controllers carrying a low-dimensional latent state and less parameters. This however can not reliably be
shown, as degradation here also increases with worse performance on the clean plant, which in our case was true
for these controllers.

\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

We have presented that, similar to nature, a bow-tie shaped architecture can improve robustness, especially to
measurement noise. Further, we showed that combining pretrained encoders with a trainable network
can reduce both parameter count and training time, while noting common problems in relation to
architecture design. Additionally, we observed tendencies in relation to other noise types, which
open possibilities for further research here.

\section{Future Research}
\label{sec:future-research}

From where this thesis ends, there are different directions, which we have left for future research
and discussion. Some, but not only limited to include whether:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item And how the number of parameters can be more effectively distributed between
	the extractor and the actor, to achieve comparable performances to a fully-connected network
	\item Better robustness to measurement noise for low-dimensional controllers also holds
	for more complex systems, such a walking humanoid roboter
	\item These findings are transferrable to real-world applications, i.e. if this setup
	would be constructed and tested
	\item Fully-connected networks improve robustness to control noise,
	and low-dimensional networks robustness to parametric uncertainty
\end{enumerate}

Finally, we also want to mention, that while these are interesting directions, the data produced from
our experiments still offers a lot of possibilities for additional analysis. For example,
while we captured the compute time and environment steps needed, a different angle could include
this in the evaluation of the approaches. And while it seems like our conclusions
leave us with more questions than we started with, this is what research is for.
